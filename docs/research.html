Figure 1 Parent and Variant Comparison

(Did protein engineering measurably improve methanol tolerance over the wild-type parent?)

a: For nine wild-type lipases, estimated methanol-tolerance gain of the best engineered variant vs its parent (ΔMTS = WT − variant; positive = variant more tolerant), 95% credible intervals; all nine cross zero (0/9 credible) — no variant is credibly more tolerant than its parent. b: RML N-glycosylation series ranked by estimated gain over Pro-RML (N314 highest to N80 lowest); markers are point estimates, dashed lines paired 95% intervals from the joint covariance; wide overlapping intervals mean the ranking should not be over-read.

Figure 2 Construct Score Spread

(How methanol-tolerance scores of all constructs are distributed and cluster by enzyme family.)

a: All 439 constructs ordered most-to-least tolerant (rank 1 = most tolerant) by frozen score with uncertainty intervals; colour = rank-stability tier: green robust tolerant (37), red robust fragile (38), grey uncertain (364). b: Same scores grouped by enzyme family with family-median box and per-family robust-tolerant (RT) / robust-fragile (RF) counts; robust-tolerant constructs concentrate in a few engineered families while most families sit in the uncertain middle — family identity, not per-construct detail, organizes the landscape.

Figure 3 Cross-study Agreement

(How reproducible the measurements are — and the ceiling on prediction precision.)

a: Paired same-construct/condition measurements; dashed diagonal = perfect agreement; same-study pairs agree tightly (r=0.95), different-study pairs scatter more (r=0.86). b: Same for derived metric D_obs (within-study r=0.91, cross-study r=0.77). c: Bland–Altman for residual activity, difference vs mean; red dashed = 95% limits of agreement; cross-study band ≈ ±28%. d: Bland–Altman for D_obs; cross-study limits ≈ ±0.85; c–d define a reproducibility ceiling: no model can predict tolerance more precisely than the data can be reproduced.
