New enzyme entry

Query a new enzyme by sequence

Paste one protein sequence (FASTA or plain residues). MeTLA aligns it against the 124 sequence-bearing entries in this release and returns three things: the closest already-measured lipases with their MTS and measurement conditions, experimentally observed mutation positions transferred onto your numbering, and the seven engineering routes with their cross-parent recurrence counts.

This page does not predict methanol tolerance. It reports what has already been measured on related sequences, and where those experiments were done. It returns no tolerance estimate for your sequence, and a transferred position is a place somebody else has already mutated — not a recommendation that changing it will help you.

1 · Paste a sequence

Runs entirely in your browser. Nothing is uploaded, and no sequence leaves this page.

2 · Closest already-measured enzymes

Ranked by Smith–Waterman local alignment identity multiplied by the fraction of the library sequence covered, so a short high-identity fragment does not outrank a full-length homologue. A lower MTS means higher modelled tolerance at the unified reference condition; read it with its uncertainty range and its support, never on its own.

3 · Candidate positions transferred onto your sequence

Positions, not predictions. Each row means: on a related enzyme, somebody mutated this position and reported a result. The sign of that result is shown, including the rows where the change made tolerance worse. Counterexamples are listed in the same table on purpose — do not read only the green ones.

4 · Seven engineering routes and how often they recur

Counted over 13 independent parent enzymes in the frozen mechanism map. "Supported" and "counterexample" are not mutually exclusive: a parent can appear in both columns, which is why they do not sum to the denominator. These are descriptive occurrence counts — they are not success rates, and they do not rank the routes for your enzyme.

What this page cannot do

  • No point estimate of tolerance for your sequence. Most displayable constructs enter the fit with only one or two observations, 96% of the corpus is single-source, structural family is not significant once experimental conditions are fixed (permutation p ≈ 0.08), and single-variable rules built from residue physicochemistry reach 0.72 accuracy under parent-holdout on 119 constructs — the same 0.72 that randomised labels reach. There is no calibrated model to expose, so none is exposed.
  • Only 124 of 671 entries are searchable. The alignment index holds exactly the entries with a release-scoped protein sequence; 70 of those also carry a public MTS. A neighbour that exists in the atlas but has no sequence in this release cannot be found from this page.
  • Only 22 mutation positions, on 8 parents, are transferable at all. A reported mutation is only usable here when its numbering was verified residue-by-residue against the parent sequence. Most reported constructs in the atlas fail that check (literature or mature-chain numbering that could not be resolved, or no construct definition), so they are excluded rather than guessed. Every one of the 22 transferable positions rests on a single source.
  • The exact reference condition is thin. At exactly 50% methanol / 2 h / 25 °C there are only three independent wild-type enzymes (Candida antarctica lipase B, Bacillus subtilis lipase A, and Proteus mirabilis lipase). Everything else at that condition is a construct or a chemically modified preparation of those three. The assays also differ: C16 p-nitrophenyl palmitate versus C4 p-nitrophenyl butyrate, at pH 7.0, pH 7.5, or unreported. This is not a 17-enzyme comparison set.
  • Sequence similarity is not mechanism. A high identity hit tells you the two proteins are related. It does not tell you the methanol response transfers, and the atlas contains same-parent pairs where neighbouring substitutions at one position go in opposite directions.

How to make your measurement comparable

Report a ratio, not an absolute. When you assay a new enzyme, run a commercial yardstick lipase in parallel, under your own conditions, and report your enzyme's residual activity relative to that yardstick alongside the raw numbers.

Why: for the same object measured in different papers, the spread in reported residual activity has a median range of 18 percentage points; restricting to a single source brings that down to 6. Meanwhile only 51 groups in the whole corpus measured two or more different wild-type lipases in the same paper under the same condition, the largest such group holds 3 enzymes, and together they cover just 26 wild types. A relative number travels between labs; an absolute percentage largely does not.
